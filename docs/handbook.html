Course Manager

How to use the study tracker

Everything the app does, in the order you meet it — from the three keystrokes that open it to the two timers that log your hours.

Runs at localhost:3000 Opens with Windows → CMG → Enter Data stays on this machine

Opening it

Three keystrokes. You never need to find the folder or start a terminal.

  1. Press Windows The Start menu search opens.
  2. Type CMG “CMG – Course Manager” appears at the top of the results.
  3. Press Enter The browser opens at the Overview. If the server was not already running, it starts silently first and the page waits for it.

If a server is already running — because you left npm start open in a terminal — CMG uses that one rather than starting a second. Two copies never share the database.

The two panes

Every screen is a sidebar for choosing what to look at, and a workspace that shows it.

Sidebar
Overview at the top, then every course with its completion bar underneath. Click a course to open it. The bars update the moment you tick something off.
Top bar
The sidebar toggle on the left, the action for the current page in the middle (+ New course on the Overview, ← All courses inside one), then the pomodoro clock and the distraction-free button.
Distraction-free
Hides the sidebar and widens the reading area. Esc brings it back.
Confirmations
Every delete asks first, in a dialog inside the page. Cancel is already focused, and Esc cancels — so a stray Enter never destroys anything.

The Overview

The landing page, and a read on where things stand across every course at once.

Continue
The card at the very top, pointing at the last course and chapter you had open. One click puts you back. Opening the app never jumps you there on its own — you get the Overview first, and go back when you choose to.
Upcoming
Deadlines and reminders from every course, closest first. Anything past its date is flagged overdue and anything inside three days due soon. Nothing is hidden for being late.
Study this week
Hours logged over the last seven days. Pick a course from the dropdown and press Start studying to run a timer without opening the course first.
Activity
A year of days. A square darkens with both the sub-lessons you finished and the hours you logged that day, so a day of only reading and a day of only ticking boxes each show up.
Course cards
One per course: how many sub-lessons are done, the completion bar, and any deadline flags. Click through to the course.
Weekly report
The quiet button at the top right downloads a PDF: every course's progress, the hours you put in this week, what is due, and which course needs the most attention.

Why the grid starts blank

The app only began recording which day a sub-lesson was finished when the activity grid was built. Lessons you ticked off before that have no date attached, so they draw nothing — guessing a day would paint activity onto days you did not work. The grid fills in from your next tick onward.

The outline

A course holds chapters, and each chapter holds sub-lessons. Click a chapter heading to fold it away; its count and bar stay visible either way. Each sub-lesson carries a status marker — a hollow outline while it is outstanding, a check once it is done. Click the marker or the title to switch it.

Ticking one thing updates five: the marker, the chapter's count, the chapter's bar, the ring at the top of the page and the bar beside the course in the sidebar. Percentages are shown to one decimal, so 37.5% means exactly three of eight.

Add a sub-lesson from the box at the foot of its chapter, and a chapter from the box at the foot of the list. Both append to the end. Rename and Delete sit on every row.

Deadlines and reminders

A deadline is a title and a date — a report, an exam. A reminder is a note to yourself with a date, and a checkbox to tick when it is handled. Both use the same colours as the Overview: overdue once the date has passed, due soon inside three days. Overdue items stay on the list rather than quietly disappearing.

Notes & resources

Free text under the whole course, newest first. Paste a link and it becomes clickable. Edit and Delete sit on each note.

Tracking time

There are two clocks and they work differently. Both write into the same study log; neither one rounds in your favour.

The study timer

In a course's Study time panel, or straight from the Overview. Press Start studying and choose how long you are sitting down for — 15, 25, 45 minutes or a number of your own. It counts down.

When it reaches zero it freezes and asks rather than logging on its own. A timer hits zero exactly when nobody is at the desk, and a clock left running through that would credit you an hour of studying to an empty room. Add time extends it and carries on from the total you had banked; Stop and log ends the session and writes it down. Dismissing the question decides nothing — the timer stays frozen and the course page keeps offering you both.

Stopping early logs what actually ran, not what you aimed for. The timer keeps going while you move between courses, but it lives in memory only: reloading the page loses a running timer.

The pomodoro

The clock in the top bar. Press play, choose a focus length, and it runs focus → a five-minute break, with a fifteen-minute break after every fourth. Only a completed focus interval logs a study session, dated to when that interval actually ended — so one that finishes at 23:58, or while the app was shut, lands on the day you worked it.

It credits whichever course is open at the moment the interval finishes, so starting on the Overview and walking into a course still works. With no course open it says so and logs nothing. The next interval is always left stopped, never auto-started. Unlike the study timer, the pomodoro survives a reload.

Do not run both at once

The two clocks do not know about each other. Running the study timer and the pomodoro over the same stretch logs that time twice, and your weekly total will be wrong. Pick one per sitting.

Logging by hand

The form under the timer takes a date, a number of hours and an optional note — for a session you did away from the app. Hours accept decimals, up to 24 in a day.

Keyboard

Three shortcuts. They stand down while you are typing in a field.

N
New course. Works on the Overview, where the button carries the same hint; inside a course it does nothing.
Ctrl + B
Show or hide the sidebar.
Esc
Close the dialog that is open. With none open, leaves distraction-free mode.

Your data

One file, on this machine, and nothing else.

  • Everything lives in data/courses.db — courses, chapters, sub-lessons, notes, deadlines, reminders and every logged session.
  • To back it up, stop the server first, then copy the file. Copying it while the app is running can capture a half-written database.
  • Deleting a course removes everything under it, which is why that confirmation names what is about to go.
  • There is no account and no sync. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Dates throughout are plain calendar days read against your local clock, not timestamps — so a deadline on the 6th is the 6th here, whatever the timezone.
